# Colour direction (D09)

Decide colour once for the whole video (a **colour script**), then per scene, then per shot. Never pick colours shot by shot.

## The colour script
```
Base palette:    two or three colours that define the world (with hex for digital)
Accent colour:   one colour reserved for the core idea / the key object
Motif colour:    a colour tied to a person, place or idea; returns at the callback
Progression:     act 1 -> act 2 -> act 3 (for example cool and desaturated -> mixed -> warm)
Contrast plan:   where saturation and contrast peak (the emotional peak)
Continuity:      one grade per scene group; same skin tones across cuts
```
Meaning is defined inside the video. Do not claim a universal meaning ("red = danger"); write "in this video, red = the attacker's presence". Colour associations depend on culture and context.

## Techniques

### Complementary palette
What: opposite colours (orange/teal, red/green). Effect: strong separation, energy, two forces.
Use when: two worlds or two sides; separating skin from background. Avoid when: every frame (the "orange-teal" cliché). Source: craft convention.

### Analogous palette
What: neighbouring colours. Effect: harmony, calm, unity.
Use when: a peaceful or nostalgic world. Avoid when: you need contrast between forces. Source: craft convention.

### Monochromatic palette
What: one hue in many values. Effect: mood immersion, coldness or dream.
Use when: a strong single mood; a flashback or a separate strand. Avoid when: the viewer must distinguish many elements. Source: craft convention.

### Warm and cool tones
What: temperature of the palette. Effect: set inside the video (home vs outside, human vs machine, past vs present).
Use when: a warm-cool contrast carries the story turn. Avoid when: unmotivated shifts between shots. Source: craft convention.

### Saturation
What: intensity of colour. Effect: high = vitality, pop, commercial; low = realism, memory, exhaustion.
Use when: raise it toward the emotional peak or the product hero; lower it for harsh reality. Avoid when: maximum saturation everywhere. Source: craft convention.

### Contrast (tonal)
What: distance between dark and light. Effect: drama and clarity rise with contrast.
Use when: follows the tension arc (with lighting). Avoid when: crushed blacks hide information. Source: craft convention.

### Colour grading
What: adjusting colour and tone in post (Resolve: node-based, colour-managed workflow - S24). Effect: unifies shots, sets the look.
Use when: always - balance first (exposure, white balance, skin), then the look. Avoid when: a look that fights skin tones. Source: S24.

### Film emulation
What: a grade that imitates film stock (curve, colour response, grain, halation). Effect: texture, period, warmth (S12: Dune printed to film and scanned back).
Use when: a period or organic identity. Avoid when: clean tech/product looks. Source: S12.

### Colour continuity
What: the same scene keeps the same white balance, palette and skin tone across cuts. Effect: invisible editing.
Use when: always within a scene. Avoid when: -. Source: craft convention.

### Colour progression and motifs
What: the palette changes across the video with the story; a motif colour marks an idea. Effect: the viewer feels the arc and links ideas by colour without being told (S15: Doyle - red appears in Wong Kar-wai's films only from In the Mood for Love; S11: Dune's infrared look for one world).
Use when: a story with a clear arc or several worlds. Avoid when: changes nobody can read. Source: S11, S15.

## House design systems
If the project has a design system (fonts, palette, card style), the colour script uses its palette and only defines the accent, motif and progression on top.
