# Cinematographer library - techniques and the reasons behind them

Rule: cite only what a source says. A cinematographer is not an "aesthetic" to copy; each entry is a technique tied to a story reason. Use these as precedents in "Decisions explained", never as a style preset. Source ids: `references/research_sources.md`.

| DP | Film | Technique | Story reason | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roger Deakins | 1917 (2019) | the film plays as one continuous shot; stabilised rigs (Steadicam, Trinity, Technocrane, vehicles) | make the audience feel the soldiers' urgency by staying with them every step | S09 |
| Roger Deakins | 1917 | one 40 mm Signature Prime for almost everything; 35 mm in tunnels and bunkers | consistent perspective; a wider lens to make tight spaces feel more claustrophobic | S09 |
| Roger Deakins | 1917 | Alexa Mini LF large format for a little less depth of field | matched the feel of First World War photographs | S09 |
| Roger Deakins | 1917 | avoided long stretches behind an actor's back unless something important was ahead | keep the audience connected to the characters | S09 |
| Roger Deakins | 1917 | overcast skies for 360° moves; practical lamps and flashlights in bunkers showing "just enough" | no room for lights in all-round moves; darkness keeps the danger | S09 |
| Emmanuel Lubezki | The Revenant (2015) | natural light and firelight only | physical truth of the world | S10 |
| Emmanuel Lubezki | The Revenant | very wide lenses close to the actors; Steadicam; long takes (building on Birdman) | presence and immersion | S10 |
| Greig Fraser | Dune: Part Two (2024) | Alexa 65, framed for IMAX 1.43:1 | scale of the desert world | S11 |

## How to use a precedent
"Push-in on the realisation, single lens family" -> cite the principle, not the film's look: "consistent perspective, one lens change only at the turn (precedent: 1917, 35 mm in the tunnels, S09)".
