# Shot sequencing and visual rhythm (D11)

Shots are judged in sequence, not alone. When a cut must sacrifice something, keep the higher priority (Murch, S21): **emotion > story > rhythm > eye trace > 2D screen plane > 3D space.**

## Shot duration
A shot lasts as long as the viewer needs to take in what is new, plus the emotional hold - no more. Editors call the time needed to register a shot its **content curve**: a simple image (a smile) has a short curve; several planes of action or text to read have a long one (S19).
- New information to read (text, diagram, object): enough time to read it at least once (about 3 words per second for on-screen text is a craft convention; test it).
- Emotional beats: hold after the peak (a held shot says "this matters").
- Repetition or familiar images: shorter.
- Short-form: the cut cadence follows ideas and sentences, not a fixed timer.

## Pacing
Pacing = shot length + movement inside the frame + information density + sound. Build pace towards the peak (shorter shots, closer sizes), then release (longer, wider). Vary pace: constant fast is as flat as constant slow.

## Cuts and transitions

### Continuity editing
What: cuts that hide themselves by keeping space, time and direction clear (180°, 30°, eyelines, match on action). Effect: the viewer follows the story, not the editing.
Use when: default for narrative and explainers. Avoid when: a style needs visible editing. Source: S17, S31.

### Match cut
What: a cut between two shots that share a shape, movement or idea. Effect: links two ideas or times strongly.
Use when: a metaphor or time jump with a real link. Avoid when: the link is forced. Source: craft convention.

### Graphic match
What: shapes, colours or positions match across the cut. Effect: visual rhyme.
Use when: motifs, callbacks, transitions in explainers (a circle becomes a lock). Avoid when: -. Source: craft convention.

### Cut on action
What: cut during a movement, continued in the next shot. Effect: the movement hides the cut.
Use when: changing size or angle during an action. Avoid when: the action is unclear in either shot. Source: craft convention.

### J-cut and L-cut
What: J = the next shot's sound starts before its picture; L = this shot's sound continues over the next picture. Effect: flow, anticipation (J), reflection (L).
Use when: conversations, interviews, narration over B-roll. Avoid when: the sound and picture must land together for a hit. Source: craft convention.

### Jump cut
What: a cut within the same shot framing, skipping time. Effect: energy, impatience, time passing, raw style.
Use when: vlogs, Shorts presenters (removing pauses), a deliberate style. Avoid when: invisible continuity is required. Source: S31.

### Cross-cutting / parallel editing
What: alternate between two actions in different places. Effect: suspense (will they meet?), comparison.
Use when: two lines of action converge or contrast; cut in a regular, rhythmic pattern to build dread (S19: Way Down East). Fluent audiences can be surprised by revealing a third line (S19). Avoid when: the viewer cannot tell the places apart (give each its own colour/geography). Source: S19.

### Montage
What: a sequence of short shots that compresses time or builds an idea. Effect: progress, accumulation, emotion.
Use when: training, travel, time passing, a list of examples. Avoid when: it replaces a scene that needs to play. Source: craft convention.

### Hard cut
What: a direct cut with no effect. Effect: clean, honest, fast.
Use when: default. Avoid when: -. Source: craft convention.

### Transitions (dissolve, fade, wipe, whip)
What: visible changes between shots. Effect: dissolve = time passing or connection; fade to black = an ending or a chapter; wipe = style; whip = energy with a spatial link.
Use when: the transition has a meaning. Avoid when: decoration; house rule: no swish/whoosh sound on transitions unless the user asks (S29). Source: craft convention; S29.

### Visual rhythm
What: the pattern of shot lengths, sizes, movement and brightness over time. Effect: the viewer feels the beat of the piece.
Use when: plan rhythm on the emotion timeline; in M08, align cuts to musical phrases, not every beat. Avoid when: the same size-angle-length pattern repeats (principle 13). Source: S15 (Doyle: movement built on music); craft convention.

### Temporal continuity
What: time in the story is clear across cuts (same moment, later, flashback). Effect: understanding.
Use when: mark time jumps (light change, a dissolve, a title, a costume change). Avoid when: unmarked jumps that make the viewer guess the order. Source: craft convention.

### Visual callbacks
What: a later shot echoes an earlier one with a change. Effect: meaning by comparison, closure.
Use when: endings; the motif register lists them. Avoid when: a callback with no change (it only repeats). Source: craft convention.

### Kuleshov effect (juxtaposition)
What: the same shot means different things after different shots - viewers projected hunger, grief and desire onto one unchanged face (S19). Effect: meaning comes from the pair, not the single shot.
Use when: a reaction must read a certain way (cut it after the cause); in AI or archive work, a neutral face plus the right cutaway carries the emotion. Avoid when: the juxtaposition implies something untrue in documentary. Source: S19.

### Master and coverage strategy
What: shoot the whole scene wide (master), then again closer from several angles (coverage); build the scene from both (S19). Effect: freedom in the edit; geography stays clear.
Use when: dialogue and blocking-heavy scenes. Cut back to the master to reset the tone or the geography after a clash; move to close coverage when the scene reaches its thematic question (S19, Whiplash). Avoid when: a planned long take or a one-shot idea carries the scene. Source: S19.

### Ellipsis
What: a cut that removes time the story does not need (car arrives -> she is already at the door) (S19). Effect: pace without confusion.
Use when: always - show only the moments that carry story. Avoid when: the removed time is itself the point (waiting, effort). Source: S19.

### Discontinuity editing
What: cuts that break continuity on purpose: jump cuts, reversed screen direction, fragmented order (S19: Breathless, The Limey). Effect: a fractured mind, chaos, a comment on watching.
Use when: the theme is fracture, memory or disorientation, and the choice is consistent. Avoid when: it is an accident of missing coverage. Source: S19.

## Sequence patterns (choose, do not repeat blindly)
- Wide -> medium -> close (orientation then emotion) - classic, use for new scenes.
- Close -> wide (reveal context, surprise) - use when the context is the twist.
- Insert -> reaction (cause -> effect).
- POV -> reaction -> POV closer (discovery).
- Static -> moving -> static (stillness frames the moment of change).
Record the pattern per sequence; change it when two sequences in a row use the same one.
